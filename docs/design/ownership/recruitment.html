Recruitment

How a player makes a companion their own: they pay its price in emeralds.

In game

Right-click

  1. Sneaking lets the held item act instead, as on the companion's own screen (The companion's screen).
  2. An operator with the companion debug stick in the clicking hand gets the companion's screen with the Debug tab, whether it has an owner or not (Companion debug stick). In anyone else's hand the stick is any item.
  3. A companion without an owner: anyone who right-clicks it gets its recruitment screen.
  4. Its owner gets the companion's screen, as before.
  5. A companion that has an owner cannot be recruited: for anyone but its owner, a right-click does nothing.
DecisionA right-click on a companion that has no owner opens its recruitment screen, whatever the player holds, without the companion debug stick. Sneaking lets the held item act instead.
DecisionA companion that has an owner cannot be recruited. For anyone but its owner a right-click does nothing, as before.

The recruitment screen

DecisionThe recruitment screen is its own screen, not a tab of the companion's screen: it is what a stranger sees before the companion is theirs. Everything it shows of the companion is read-only.
  1. Its armor and its two designated weapons, as icons: nothing can be taken or put there; hovering one shows the item's tooltip.
  2. Its view in 3D, as on the companion's screen.
  3. Its name with the icon of its group, its health and armor bars, as on the companion's screen (The companion's screen).
  4. The Recruit button, which carries the price: the label on the left, the price on the right, drawn as a villager's offer draws it, an emerald with the count over its bottom right corner. Here the drawn price, 24, is struck through in red, and the price this player pays, 22, sits beside it (Reputation).
  5. The inventory of the player who opened the screen.
  6. The drawer, whose only panel is the attributes one, with its one handle. Its storage is not shown.
DecisionRecruit is the mod's own button, in the screen's colours (the accent, as the screen's main action), not vanilla's: the label on the left, the price on the right, drawn as a villager's offer draws it, an emerald with the count over its bottom right corner. A lowered or raised price shows the drawn price struck through in red and the price to pay beside it, as a villager's offer does (MerchantScreen.java:224-238).
Ruled outWhy
A price line under the heartsIt did not read as the main information

The price

DecisionThe price is a number of emeralds, drawn once when the companion is created, uniformly between 12 and 36, saved with it and never drawn again. It has no cap, unlike a villager's offers (64 at most): once the equipment counts, a price may pass a stack. It does not depend on the equipment yet: a scale of what each piece of equipment adds comes with the equipment companions spawn with.

For example, a companion drawn at 24 keeps 24 as its drawn price for as long as it has no owner, whatever it is given to wear.

DecisionThe player pays the drawn price lowered or raised by the companion's opinion of them and by Hero of the Village, as at a villager (Reputation). The same companion drawn at 24 costs 22 to a player it thinks well of, and 27 to one who hit it today.

Paying

DecisionThe player clicks Recruit, and the price is taken from their inventory. The button is greyed while they have fewer emeralds than the price, with a tooltip that says how many it takes.
Ruled outWhy
A payment slot, where the player puts the emeralds first, as in a beacon, and what is left goes back to the inventory when the screen closesTried in game and ruled out. A price above 64 would also have needed more than one slot
DecisionA player in creative mode pays too, as with villagers.

Sound

DecisionRecruiting plays no sound yet; it will get a sound of the mod's own.
FactVanilla plays none when a villager's trade concludes: the "yes" sounds when the payment is placed (MerchantContainer.java:122), and the "ting" is the experience orb dropped after it.
Ruled outWhy
A vanilla sound: the experience orb, level up, challenge complete, the villager's and the wandering trader's "yes", item pickup, bundle insertEach was heard in game and ruled out

Once paid

DecisionOnce the player has paid, the companion is theirs, and that is all: it does not follow them, it has no mode yet (Ownership and modes).
DecisionRight after the payment, the recruitment screen closes and the companion's screen opens, now that the player is its owner.
DecisionWhen two players have the screen of the same companion open, the first to pay recruits it; the other's screen closes.

How it is built

  1. The client predicts what a right-click does from the synced owner (ClientCompanion.interact): here, no owner.
  2. The server's Companion.interact finds no owner and opens RecruitMenu through Fabric's ExtendedMenuType. The opening data carry the companion's entity id, its drawn price and the price this player pays (Reputation), and a copy of its armor and designated weapons, taken when the screen opens.
  3. While the screen is open, the server sends the attributes, as for the companion's screen. The client greys Recruit with the same test as the server.
  4. Recruit is a menu button: vanilla calls clickMenuButton only for the open menu, still valid.
  5. The server checks the emeralds again, and takes them.
  6. It sets the owner, which every client that sees the companion receives.
  7. Another player's recruitment menu is no longer valid, since the companion has an owner: their screen closes.
  8. The server opens the companion's screen for the player who paid, which closes the recruitment one.

The owner

DecisionThe owner is a persistent attachment of the companion, synced to every client that sees it, as its identity (Identity, names and skins): the owner's UUID and their name at the time of recruitment. The client needs it to predict a right-click (recruitment screen, companion's screen or nothing) and to show the owner on the companion's screen. Companion.isOwner reads it.
QuestionThe owner's name when they change it on their account.

The price

DecisionThe price is a persistent attachment, drawn in the constructor on the server through getAttachedOrCreate, as the identity (Companion.java:72-80).
FactFabric keeps the constructor's attachments when the saved entity has none, and replaces them by the saved ones otherwise (AttachmentTargetsMixin, read in the bytecode of fabric-data-attachment-api 2.2.35), so a companion keeps its price through /summon and through saving.

The screen

DecisionIts own container menu (RecruitMenu) and screen, opened with Fabric's ExtendedMenuType. The opening data carry the companion's entity id, its drawn price and the price to pay (Reputation), and a copy of the armor and designated weapons: vanilla sends a client the armor of an entity it sees, but not the designated weapons, which are inventory slots (Inventory). The copy is taken when the screen opens.
DecisionThe pieces both screens show are shared rather than copied: the 3D view, the name heading, the health and armor bars, the drawer and its attributes panel, and the server's attributes message: a base menu (CompanionViewMenu) and a base screen (CompanionViewScreen) hold them.
DecisionThe menu's slots are the player's inventory only; shift-click moves nothing. Its validity: the companion alive, within 4 blocks as the companion's screen, and still without an owner.
DecisionRecruit is a menu button (clickMenuButton), which vanilla calls only for the open menu, still valid (ServerGamePacketListenerImpl.java:2046-2058). The server checks the emeralds again, takes them, sets the owner, then opens the companion's screen for the player, which closes the recruitment one. The client greys the button with the same test, as the enchanting table does (EnchantmentMenu.java:135-148, EnchantmentScreen.java:112). Emeralds are counted and taken from the player's 36 inventory slots, the ones the menu shows, so that client and server count the same.

Right-click

DecisionCompanion.interact opens the recruitment screen for anyone when the companion has no owner, the companion's screen for its owner, and does nothing for anyone else; sneaking, and the debug stick in an operator's hand, let the click pass as today. ClientCompanion.interact predicts the same from the synced owner.
