Reputation

What a companion thinks of a player, and how it lowers or raises the price of its recruitment (Recruitment).

In game

DecisionA companion has its own opinion of each player, made of the same rumours as a villager's, with the same numbers, the same forgetting and the same word of mouth. The rumour system is taken as it is, unchanged.

The rumours

FactHow a villager's opinion works, in vanilla 26.3: five kinds of rumour, each held per player with a value, weighed, capped, forgotten a little each day, and losing some of its value when passed on.

The opinion is the sum of each rumour's value times its weight, from −700 to +150. Two villagers who meet pass rumours on, one way, at most once a minute each, in the morning, the late afternoon and around the bell; the good spreads badly and the bad spreads well.

What a companion hears

DecisionWhat a companion's opinion hears:
EventRumour, as for a villager
A player hits itMinor negative +25
A player kills a villager or a companion in front of itMajor negative +25
A villager passes rumours on to itAs between two villagers: up to 10, with their losses

It cannot trade, but trading rumours reach it by word of mouth, as any other.

  1. A player who hits the companion adds a minor negative rumour of 25 against them, as on a villager.
  2. A player who kills a villager or a companion in front of it adds a major negative rumour of 25: it is a witness as a villager is.
  3. A villager close by passes rumours on to it as to another villager: up to 10, each losing its amount for being passed on (see the table of rumours). This is the only way trading rumours, and the cure's minor positive one, reach a companion; the cure's major positive one never spreads.
  4. Every day, each rumour loses its daily amount, until it is gone. A major positive rumour is never forgotten.
  5. When a player opens its recruitment screen, the price they pay is computed from the companion's opinion of them (see the price below).
  6. A companion killed in front of villagers counts for them as a villager killed: their own prices go up for the killer, and they pass the rumour on, to companions too.
DecisionKilling a companion in front of villagers counts for them as killing a villager: their own prices go up for the killer.

The price

DecisionThe price of a recruitment follows the opinion as a villager's offer does, with a coefficient of 0.1, between the villagers' 0.05 (most offers) and 0.2 (armour, tools, books): the opinion × 0.1, rounded down, comes off the price. A bad opinion raises it, by at least 1, as a villager's.
FactA companion's good opinion stays small. It learns the good only by word of mouth: the major positive rumour of a cure never spreads, and hearing several villagers keeps the strongest version of each rumour rather than adding them (GossipContainer.java:161-163). At best it holds a minor positive 20 (from a cured villager) and trading 5: +25, so 2 emeralds off. A bad opinion goes down to −700, 70 more.
DecisionKept at 0.1: the rumour system makes the good spread badly, and that is accepted; Hero of the Village gives the larger discounts.
DecisionHero of the Village lowers it too, as at a villager: by 30 % (level I) to 55 % (level V) of the drawn price, at least 1, on top of the opinion.
DecisionThe price never drops below 1, and has no cap (Recruitment). It is computed when the recruitment screen opens.

How it is built

  1. The companion is a ReputationEventHandler, the interface through which a dying villager tells its witnesses (Villager.java:597-606). A hit on the companion (setLastHurtByMob) tells itself only, as a villager's; its death tells its witnesses (villagers and companions within 16 blocks that see it), through the same interface, with vanilla's event types.
  2. The rumour an event adds is the villager's rule, copied (Villager.onReputationEventFrom, Villager.java:858-868).
  3. It hears a villager standing within √5 blocks of it, as two villagers do, while that villager is idle or meeting, at most once a minute, through transferFrom.
  4. decay runs once a day of game time.
  5. The price is the villager's rule, copied (Villager.updateSpecialPrices, Villager.java:409-424), computed by the server when the recruitment screen opens.
  6. The client receives the drawn price and the price to pay with the screen's opening data, never the rumours.

DecisionThe companion uses vanilla's rumour pieces themselves, so that a mod changing them changes the companions too: a GossipContainer of its own, saved with the entity as a villager saves its own (GossipContainer.CODEC), the GossipType values, transferFrom to hear a villager, decay once a day of game time, and ReputationEventHandler, the interface through which a dying villager tells its witnesses (Villager.java:597-606).
FactTwo of a villager's rules are private code, and the companion copies them, so a mod changing them does not reach the companions: the rumour an event adds (Villager.onReputationEventFrom, Villager.java:858-868) and the price of an offer (Villager.updateSpecialPrices, Villager.java:409-424).
DecisionThis is accepted.

Events

DecisionThe companion is a ReputationEventHandler. A villager dying in front of it tells it as it tells a villager, with nothing more in the mod. A hit on it (setLastHurtByMob) tells itself only, as a villager's; its death tells its witnesses (villagers and companions within 16 blocks that see it), through the same interface, with vanilla's event types.

Hearing a villager

DecisionIt hears a villager standing within √5 blocks of it, as two villagers do (TradeWithVillager.java:48-50), while that villager is idle or meeting (its brain's activity, so a mod changing the villagers' schedule changes when companions hear), at most once a minute (1200 ticks, Villager.java:813-815).
FactA companion does not move yet, so it hears only the villagers who come close.

The client

FactThe client never receives rumours; the server computes the price and sends the drawn price and the price to pay with the screen's opening data.
