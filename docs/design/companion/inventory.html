Inventory

A companion carries as much as a player: storage, armor and two designated weapons. It drops everything when it dies.

In game

A companion carries as much as a player: 36 slots of storage, four armor slots, and two designated weapon slots, primary and secondary. It picks nothing up yet.

DecisionNo hotbar and no selected slot. A player chooses what they hold; what a companion holds is its own choice, made by its brain (not designed yet, Behaviour): it will take in hand what it uses (its designated weapon to fight, food to eat) and put it back after. Until then its hands stay empty.
DecisionThe designated weapon slots are not its hands. Each takes one weapon or one shield, and either slot takes either: a shield can be the primary. A weapon is something that deals damage: swords, axes, spears, the mace, the trident, bows and crossbows. Other mods' weapons and shields count when they are tagged as such, as almost all are (Farmer's Delight's skillet included); a modpack adds the others with a datapack (What counts as a weapon). Tools (pickaxes, shovels, hoes) are not weapons.
DecisionThe armor it wears shows on it in the world, as on a player. Nothing else of its inventory shows.
DecisionWhen it dies, it drops everything on the ground, as a player does: storage, armor, designated weapons, and its hands once it holds something. keepInventory does not apply to companions: a player keeps their items because they respawn, and a companion does not (Death). Items with the curse of vanishing are destroyed, as for a player.
DecisionNo block takes items from a companion: a hopper under it leaves it alone.
DecisionThe inventory screen shows the companion in 3D following the mouse, its armor slots, its two designated weapon slots and its 36 slots, above the inventory of the player who opened it; items move both ways. For now only an operator opens it, with the companion debug stick (Companion debug stick). It is drawn with the kit of the mod's config screens (part of the socle, the base code mod update maintains), with no texture of the mod: a scaffolding look, which may change while testing.
  1. Its four armor slots: each takes what fits it, as for a player.
  2. The companion in 3D, turning to follow the mouse.
  3. Its two designated weapon slots, primary and secondary: one weapon or one shield each, either in either.
  4. Its 36 storage slots: anything.
  5. The inventory of the player who opened the screen: for now, an operator with the companion debug stick. Items move both ways between it and the companion's slots.
QuestionTools on my Back (in grimoire-et-sortileges, the modpack the mod is tested with) shows the tools a player carries on their back. Whether it shows a companion's, and from which slots, is not checked; it matters once a companion's weapons show.

Grave mods

DecisionNo compatibility code with grave mods. A companion drops its items through vanilla's death path, so a grave mod that one day accepts entities other than players will catch them with nothing to change here.

How it is built

The inventory lives in two places on the companion: vanilla's equipment for the armor, and a container of the mod for the rest. They travel differently.

  1. Equipment: vanilla's, which every living entity has. The armor lives there, and so do the hands.
  2. Its own container: the 36 storage slots and the two designated weapon slots, a field of the entity.
  3. Both are part of the companion's entity save, which goes wherever the companion goes (Saving).
  4. Every client that sees the companion gets its equipment, when it changes and when the companion comes into view: the facade wears the armor. It never gets the container.
  5. The client of a player with the screen open gets the companion's slots through the screen's menu, and sends back what the player moves.
  6. Hoppers and the hopper minecart take nothing from a companion: it is not a vanilla Container.
DecisionThe armor is vanilla's equipment (EntityEquipment, the slots HEAD, CHEST, LEGS, FEET), which every living entity has.
FactVanilla saves it under equipment (LivingEntity.java:768-770), sends it to the clients that see the companion when it changes and when they start seeing it (LivingEntity.java:2911-2993, ServerEntity.java:314-327), and /item reaches it as armor.* (LivingEntity.java:3925-3950). The client facade applies it with no code of ours (ClientPacketListener.java:1475-1480, then PlayerEquipment).
DecisionThe 36 storage slots and the two designated weapon slots are the companion's own container, a field of the entity, as a villager holds its own (AbstractVillager.java:60).
FactVanilla's player Inventory cannot be reused: its constructor takes a Player (Inventory.java:59-66).
DecisionThe container is saved in the companion's entity save. It goes wherever the entity save goes: in its chunk, and in its owner's entry while it is away with them (Saving), with nothing more to write.
DecisionThe container is sent to a client only through the inventory screen's menu, by vanilla's menu sync.
FactVanilla never sends a player's storage to other clients either; they get only the equipment (ServerPlayer.java:298-312).
DecisionThe companion is not a vanilla Container.
FactHoppers, and the hopper minecart, take only from entities that are one (HopperBlockEntity.java:391-393, EntitySelector.java:13).
Decision/item reaches the storage as container.0 to container.35, as on a player (Player.java:1687-1726), and the designated weapons as container.36 (primary) and container.37 (secondary), through the entity's getSlot.
FactAn entity answers no slot unless it says so (Entity.java:3577).
DecisionThe drop at death overrides dropEquipment, empty for a living entity (LivingEntity.java:1515-1516): it destroys the items with the curse of vanishing, then drops the container and the equipment, as Player.dropEquipment does without its keepInventory check (Player.java:553-568). It runs inside vanilla's dropAllDeathLoot.

What counts as a weapon

DecisionAn item tag of the mod, am_i_alone:companion_weapons, decides what the designated weapon slots accept. It is built from vanilla's tags (#minecraft:swords, #minecraft:axes, #minecraft:spears, #minecraft:enchantable/weapon) and Fabric's conventional ones (#c:tools/melee_weapon, ranged_weapon, bow, crossbow, trident, mace, shield, each with required: false). A modpack adds an item with a datapack; removing one means replacing the whole tag.
FactRead in the 26.3 data and the Fabric conventional tags 4.10.5: no tag or component says "weapon or shield" alone. The weapon component is also on pickaxes, shovels and hoes (ToolMaterial.java:57); bows and crossbows carry no weapon component, only their class ProjectileWeaponItem; the shield is marked by blocks_attacks. Vanilla has no shield or ranged weapon tag; the c: tags list vanilla items by id, so c:tools/melee_weapon misses a modded sword that is only in #minecraft:swords, which is why both families are in the tag.
FactRead in the jars of grimoire-et-sortileges: the tag accepts Farmer's Delight's knives and skillet (in #minecraft:enchantable/weapon) and ReArm's netherite bow, crossbow and shield (in c:tools/bow, crossbow, shield). No other mod of the pack adds a weapon.

Vanilla's chest screen over the 36 slots (ChestMenu, four rows)No client code, but no armor, no designated weapons, no view of the companion
A read-only screen fed by a snapshotItems must be put in and taken out from the screen
Weapons recognised by their components (weapon, blocks_attacks, ProjectileWeaponItem)Accepts pickaxes, shovels and hoes, which carry weapon too; nothing a datapack can adjust
The tag, plus components as a fallback, minus a tag of exclusionsIt gives the same result as the tag alone on vanilla and on the pack, and needs a second tag. It only adds modded weapons that no tag lists, and it lets through a modded pickaxe that no tag lists
