The companion's screen

What a right-click on a companion opens: one window with tabs, through which goes everything a player does with it.

In game

A right-click on a companion opens its screen: everything a player does with it goes through there. One window with tabs: the companion's view, armor and designated weapons, and the player's inventory below, stay in place on every tab; only the area on the right changes.

  1. The tabs. Companion and Inventory for whoever opens the screen; Debug only when an operator opened it with the companion debug stick.
  2. Its four armor slots, on every tab (Inventory).
  3. The companion in 3D, turning to follow the mouse, on every tab.
  4. Its two designated weapon slots, primary and secondary, on every tab.
  5. The area that changes with the tab. On the Companion tab: its name, its health and armor bars, its owner, and its mode once the modes exist. On the Inventory tab: its 36 storage slots. On the Debug tab: its current owner, and later a way to change it.
  6. The inventory of the player who opened the screen, on every tab.

DecisionThe armor and the designated weapons are managed from every tab, the Companion tab included; the Inventory tab only adds the storage.
DecisionThe screen shows only what works. Until the modes exist, the Companion tab has no mode list; until the owner can be changed, the Debug tab only shows the current owner.
DecisionThe mode is a list: a click opens the possible modes, one is picked (Ownership and modes). The modes themselves are not designed yet.

FactA companion cannot be leashed, as a villager: only entities that are Leashable can (Mob.java:100), and a companion is not a Mob.

Until ownership exists (Ownership and modes), no companion has an owner, so only the debug stick opens the screen.

Health and armor bars

DecisionThe health and armor bars look and behave exactly as the player's own in the HUD: the same hearts (half hearts, rows, hardcore, poisoned, withered, frozen, absorption, the blink when hit and the bounce of regeneration) and the same armor icons. They follow the companion live: a hit while the screen is open takes the hearts away as on the player's bar. A resource pack that retextures them applies.
DecisionMods that change how the HUD draws hearts or armor (Overflowing Bars, Colorful Hearts) do not apply to the companion's bars. A compatibility with Overflowing Bars comes later, as an optional adapter.

How it is built

One menu, tabs on the client

DecisionThe screen is one container menu, the one of the inventory screen grown (Inventory): armor, designated weapons, storage and the player's inventory are its slots from the first tab. Switching tabs closes nothing: the item held on the cursor stays, and the view does not move.
DecisionThe server knows the open tab. The client tells it with vanilla's menu button packet (ServerboundContainerButtonClickPacket, which the server checks against the open menu and its validity before calling clickMenuButton, ServerGamePacketListenerImpl.java:2046-2059). Outside the Inventory tab, the storage slots take and give nothing on the server.
FactHiding a slot (Slot.isActive) only acts on the client's drawing and hover (Slot.java:92-94, AbstractContainerScreen.java:133,223,268); the server's clicks, shift-click and double-click gathering ignore it. Without the check above, a modified client would reach the storage from any tab.
DecisionThe Debug tab exists only when the server opened the screen with the debug stick: the server decides it when it opens the menu and sends it with the companion's entity id (Fabric's ExtendedMenuType). The menu's validity adds: whoever opened it without the stick must still be the owner.

Opening

  1. The companion is asked first, whatever the player holds (interact).
  2. When the player sneaks, it lets the click pass: the held item acts as on any entity, a name tag renames it, a splash potion is thrown.
  3. When an operator holds the companion debug stick, it lets the click pass to the stick, which opens the screen with the Debug tab, whoever owns the companion. In anyone else's hand the stick is any item, and the branches below apply (Companion debug stick).
  4. Its owner gets the screen. Until ownership exists, no companion has one, so only the debug stick opens it.
DecisionThe companion answers the right-click itself (interact), before any item: it opens the screen for its owner and lets the click pass when the player sneaks, or holds the debug stick with the gamemaster permission. The debug stick opens the screen with the Debug tab from its own interactLivingEntity, which vanilla calls when the entity let the click pass (Player.java:806-842).

Health and armor bars

FactRead in the 26.3 code: vanilla draws the player's hearts and armor in private methods of Hud (extractHearts, extractArmor, Hud.java:817-896), tied to the camera player and to the HUD's position. Fabric API wraps their call sites in the HUD (HudMixin); Overflowing Bars and Colorful Hearts replace those HUD elements and draw for the camera player only.
DecisionThe screen draws the bars itself, with vanilla's sprites (minecraft:hud/heart/*, minecraft:hud/armor_*) and vanilla's rules (rows, row height, absorption, heart types, hardcore, low-health wobble, regeneration bounce, blink), its own blink state kept by the screen.

DecisionIts absorption travels as a Fabric synced attachment, as its identity does (Identity, names and skins).
