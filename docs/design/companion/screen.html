The companion's screen

What a right-click on a companion opens: one window, with a drawer for its storage and its attributes, through which goes everything a player does with it.

In game

A right-click on a companion opens its screen: everything a player does with it goes through there.

DecisionThe layout, on every tab, as drawn below. The same padding on all four sides of the window, as vanilla's containers (7 pixels), and between the left part and the name column. The window stays whole, drawer out or not.
  1. The tabs, above the window, drawn as a stack: the open one on top, merged into the window; the other tucked under it and under the window, with a darker face. Companion for whoever opens the screen; Debug only when an operator opened it with the companion debug stick.
  2. Its four armor slots, head to feet (Inventory).
  3. Its view in 3D. The armor slots, the view and the weapon slots have the same sizes as in the first inventory screen.
  4. Its two designated weapon slots.
  5. The name column. Its name as the main heading, with an icon of its group: pink for a woman, blue for a man; a name too long for the column ends with "…". Right under the name, its armor and health bars; under the last row of hearts, the content of the tab: on the Companion tab its owner, and its mode once the modes exist.
  6. The inventory of the player who opened the screen, centred below.
  7. The drawer, sliding out from under the window on the right, as tall as the window. Its storage panel is the player's inventory turned on its side: 4 columns of 9 rows.
  8. The handles, one per panel, stacked on the drawer's edge and sharing their border: a monochrome icon (a chest, bars), an arrow that points where the drawer will go, and a tooltip. Drawn as the tabs are: the open panel's handle on top, merged into the drawer; the other tucked under it and under the drawer, with a darker face.
  9. The attributes panel: four groups (Combat, Defence, Movement, Hazards), the headings in white, each row a short label and its value aligned right. Here with a diamond sword as primary designated weapon: attack 7 and attack speed 1.6 are green, compared to a bare hand's 1.

The drawer

ClickWhat happens
A handle, drawer inThe drawer slides out on that panel
The open panel's handleThe drawer slides back in
The other handle, drawer outThe drawer shows that panel at once, without going in
DecisionThe open panel's handle takes the drawer's colour and stands 2 pixels further out, merged into the drawer; the tooltip names the panel, or the action ("Put its inventory away") on the open one. Drawer in, both handles show an arrow pointing out; drawer out, only the open panel's handle does, pointing back. The handles are buttons: they take the keyboard focus and are read by the narrator. The drawer remembers its panel: the screen opens again with the drawer out on it. When the drawer covers the window (a narrow screen), Escape puts it away first.
DecisionThe storage panel is the player's inventory turned on its side: 4 columns of 9 rows.
DecisionThe attributes panel lists its attributes in groups, the headings in white, each row a short label and its value aligned right; hovering a row shows the attribute's full name, its current value and its base. Not "statistics": that is vanilla's screen of the player's counters.
DecisionWhat the panel shows is data, so that another mod or a datapack shows its own attributes: two registries filled by data and synced to the clients, as the skins are (Identity, names and skins). A group (am_i_alone:companion_attribute_group) has a heading and an order. An entry (am_i_alone:companion_attribute) names an attribute, or a value the mod computes (attack range is the first), its group, its order in the group, its short label (the attribute's own name when absent), its format, when it is shown (always, or only when it differs from its default) and, optionally, a colour threshold: the value the colour compares to instead of the default. An attribute with no entry is not shown. A mod adds an attribute with one file of its own; a datapack replaces an entry or a group with a file of the same name.

The mod ships these entries, in four groups (Combat, Defence, Movement, Hazards):

AttributesShown
Attack damage, attack speed, attack range (Inventory), max health, armor, armor toughness, knockback resistance, movement speedAlways
Max absorption, water and ground movement efficiency, jump, step height, oxygen, burning time, explosion knockback resistance, safe fall distance, fall damage multiplierOnly when gear or an effect changes them from their default
Camera distance, name tag distances, waypoint range, gravity, scale, air drag, friction, bounciness, interaction range, attack knockbackNo entry: they mean nothing to a player looking at a companion
DecisionValues as vanilla writes them (#.##), in one of a few formats an entry picks: the plain value, the value ×10 (knockback resistance, as item tooltips show it), a percentage of its default (movement speed), a percentage (burning time, fall damage). A value that differs from its default takes the colour of its effect: green for a gain, red for a loss. The default is the attribute's own (Attribute.getDefaultValue: 20 for max health), not the companion's base, so health raised by a command or by data shows green too. Attack damage compares to 1, a bare hand's, rather than vanilla's default of 2; attack speed compares to 1 rather than its base of 4: a sword's 1.6 is green, an axe's 0.9 red. Apothic Attributes compares to the base, and would show every weapon's speed in red. Rows that overflow the panel scroll with the wheel.
DecisionWhen the drawer out does not fit on the screen, the whole screen moves left to make room, as vanilla's recipe book moves the inventory; if even that is not enough, the drawer covers part of the window.

Tabs

Tabs above the window, for real changes of page only:

TabThe content on the right showsWho sees it
CompanionIts owner, and its mode (a list to pick from) once the modes existWhoever opens the screen
DebugDebug options: its owner, later changing itAn operator holding the companion debug stick (Companion debug stick)
DecisionThe drawer belongs to the Companion tab; switching to Debug puts it away.
DecisionTabs and handles are drawn as a stack, as vanilla's creative and recipe book tabs: the selected one on top, with its full border and relief, merged into its panel; the others underneath, tucked under it and under the panel, nearer ones in front of farther ones, with a darker face and a dimmer border and relief. Their raised relief stays: only what covers them shows they are behind.
DecisionThe screen shows only what works. Until the modes exist, the Companion tab has no mode list; until the owner can be changed, the Debug tab only shows the current owner.
DecisionThe mode is a list: a click opens the possible modes, one is picked (Ownership and modes). The modes themselves are not designed yet.

FactA companion cannot be leashed, as a villager: only entities that are Leashable can (Mob.java:100), and a companion is not a Mob.

Until ownership exists (Ownership and modes), no companion has an owner, so only the debug stick opens the screen.

Health and armor bars

DecisionThe health and armor bars look and behave exactly as the player's own in the HUD: the same hearts (half hearts, rows, hardcore, poisoned, withered, frozen, absorption, the blink when hit and the bounce of regeneration) and the same armor icons. They follow the companion live: a hit while the screen is open takes the hearts away as on the player's bar. A resource pack that retextures them applies.
DecisionMods that change how the HUD draws hearts or armor (Overflowing Bars, Colorful Hearts) do not apply to the companion's bars. A compatibility with Overflowing Bars comes later, as an optional adapter.

How it is built

One menu, tabs on the client

DecisionThe screen is one container menu, the one of the inventory screen grown (Inventory): armor, designated weapons, storage and the player's inventory are its slots. Switching tabs or moving the drawer closes nothing: the item held on the cursor stays, and the view does not move.
DecisionThe server knows the open tab and the drawer's open panel. The client tells it with vanilla's menu button packet (ServerboundContainerButtonClickPacket, which the server checks against the open menu and its validity before calling clickMenuButton, ServerGamePacketListenerImpl.java:2046-2059). Unless the storage panel is the open one, the storage slots take and give nothing on the server.
FactHiding a slot (Slot.isActive) only acts on the client's drawing and hover (Slot.java:92-94, AbstractContainerScreen.java:133,223,268); the server's clicks, shift-click and double-click gathering ignore it. Without the check above, a modified client would reach the storage with the drawer in.
FactSlot positions are fixed once the menu is built, so the storage slots sit at the drawer's open position and show only once it is fully out; the player's inventory cannot move either. The name column has room for the hearts up to 200 health; from 221 the owner line reaches the player's inventory label, from about 380 the hearts cover its slots. Companions do not get that much health for now.
DecisionThe Debug tab exists only when the server opened the screen with the debug stick: the server decides it when it opens the menu and sends it with the companion's entity id (Fabric's ExtendedMenuType). The menu's validity adds: whoever opened it without the stick must still be the owner.

Attributes on the client

FactOn the client a companion is a player (Client facade), so its attribute map is a player's (Player.createAttributes), with attributes the companion does not have and other defaults: the panel never reads the client's map. Vanilla sends only the syncable attributes (ClientboundUpdateAttributesPacket); knockback resistance is not syncable (Attributes.java:61), nor may be the attributes of other mods, and the equipment's modifiers are applied on the server only.
DecisionWhile its screen is open, the server sends the client the current and base values of every attribute that has an entry and that the companion has, in one message of the mod, again when one changes. The panel shows only those.

Opening

  1. The companion is asked first, whatever the player holds (interact).
  2. When the player sneaks, it lets the click pass: the held item acts as on any entity, a name tag renames it, a splash potion is thrown.
  3. When an operator holds the companion debug stick in the clicking hand, it lets the click pass to the stick, which opens the screen with the Debug tab, whoever owns the companion. In anyone else's hand, or in the other hand, the stick is any item, and the branches below apply (Companion debug stick).
  4. Its owner gets the screen. Until ownership exists, no companion has one, so only the debug stick opens it.
DecisionThe companion answers the right-click itself (interact), before any item: it opens the screen for its owner and lets the click pass when the player sneaks, or holds the debug stick with the gamemaster permission. The debug stick opens the screen with the Debug tab from its own interactLivingEntity, which vanilla calls when the entity let the click pass (Player.java:806-842).

Health and armor bars

FactRead in the 26.3 code: vanilla draws the player's hearts and armor in private methods of Hud (extractHearts, extractArmor, Hud.java:817-896), tied to the camera player and to the HUD's position. Fabric API wraps their call sites in the HUD (HudMixin); Overflowing Bars and Colorful Hearts replace those HUD elements and draw for the camera player only.
DecisionThe screen draws the bars itself, with vanilla's sprites (minecraft:hud/heart/*, minecraft:hud/armor_*) and vanilla's rules (rows, row height, absorption, heart types, hardcore, low-health wobble, regeneration bounce, blink), its own blink state kept by the screen.
FactWhat a client knows of a companion for the bars:

Armor valueYesARMOR is a synced attribute, with the equipment's modifiers applied on the server (AttributeMap.java:26-31)

DecisionThe companion sends its effects to the clients that see it, with vanilla's own packets (ClientboundUpdateMobEffectPacket, ClientboundRemoveMobEffectPacket), when an effect is added, updated or removed, and all of them when a client starts seeing it.
FactThe client applies them to any living entity (ClientPacketListener.java:1746-1758, 1902-1906).
DecisionIts absorption travels as a Fabric synced attachment, as its identity does (Identity, names and skins).
