Saving

The anchored form goes with its chunk; the anchor form, which no chunk holds, goes in the mod's data.

In game

A companion survives everything a player's world goes through: a restart, a crash, its owner leaving. At rest, it is saved with its area; keeping the world alive, it leaves with its owner when they disconnect and comes back with them, where it was.

How it is built

DecisionEach form is saved where vanilla keeps that kind of thing, never in the players' files. In both cases, what is saved is the form's entity save, which holds the core with the rest.
Anchored formAnchor form
WhereWith its chunk, in the world's entity filesIn the mod's data, saved with the world (a Fabric global attachment): one entry per companion, keyed by its UUID, holding its entity save, which includes its dimension and position
WhenWhen vanilla saves or unloads the chunkWhen its owner disconnects, before each world save, and when the server stops
Back in the worldWith its chunkWhen its owner connects, where it was
Entry removedNever: it has noneWhen the companion switches back to the anchored form, or is removed for good

Why the mod saves the anchor form: a player is never stored in a chunk, and vanilla only writes the files of connected players. Why its entry goes when the companion switches back: an entry left behind would bring a second companion back when its owner connects.

Step by step

  1. The owner disconnects (ServerPlayConnectionEvents.DISCONNECT): the anchor form's entity save is written to its entry, then the form leaves the world.
  2. A world save (autosave, /save-all, server stop): before vanilla writes anything (ServerLifecycleEvents.BEFORE_SAVE), the mod updates the entries of the anchor forms in the world and keeps those of the companions that are away. Vanilla then writes the chunks and the mod's data, in the same save.
  3. The owner connects (ServerPlayConnectionEvents.JOIN): for each entry of a companion they own, the mod reads the dimension and position, loads that chunk with a ticket as vanilla does for a joining player (TicketType.PLAYER_SPAWN), creates the anchor form and adds it.

Edge cases

FactMet in the prototype: at startup, vanilla saves the world before any player has joined. A save must only update the companions present in the world and keep the others; replacing the whole list would erase every companion whose owner is away.

A crash between two saves brings the chunks and the mod's data back to the same moment, since they are written in the same save. A crash while the save is being written is not covered; vanilla has the same window between the players' files and the chunks.

QuestionAn owner who never comes back. Their anchor form stays in the mod's data, out of the world, for good; whether it should come back after a while, as an anchored form without owner, is not decided.
