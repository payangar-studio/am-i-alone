The two forms

A companion is either at rest, held by its area, or keeping the world alive around it like a player.

In game

At rest (anchored), it lives like a villager: it stays in its area, is saved with it, and freezes when the area unloads. Keeping the world alive (anchor), it loads the world around it exactly like a player, with every effect of a player: it makes monsters spawn around it, and it counts for sleeping, so its owner places a bed for it too to skip the night.

DecisionA companion starts anchored. It becomes an anchor when its owner puts it in follow mode, and anchored again in another mode or without owner. What the owner and the modes decide, including what happens when the owner disconnects, is in Ownership and modes.
  1. A companion starts anchored, wherever it comes from.
  2. Its owner puts it in follow mode: it keeps the world alive around it as it goes.
  3. Another mode, or no owner any more: it is at rest again, where it stands.
  4. Its owner disconnects: it leaves the world with them.
  5. Its owner connects again: it comes back where it was.

How it is built

DecisionThe names. Anchored: held by its chunk. Anchor: holds the world loaded around it. They say what the form is to the world, not what it does for its owner: another mode than follow may take the anchor form later.

Why two: one vanilla class cannot be both.

FactRead in the 26.3 code: a world registers as one of its players any ServerPlayer it starts tracking (ServerLevel), and the chunk map loads chunks around it (ChunkMap.addEntity). A player is never stored with a chunk.
FactRead in the 26.3 code: a ServerPlayer gets its statistics and advancements from the server's player list when it is built (getPlayerStats, getPlayerAdvancements), which works outside the list; in the prototype, the anchor form ticked, loaded chunks and saved without being in it. Code that goes through the list of connected players (@a, a command naming a player) does not see it.
